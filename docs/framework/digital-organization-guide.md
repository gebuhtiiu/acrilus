# Acrilus Digital Organization Guide

## Purpose

Modern digital life produces thousands of files, documents, records, screenshots, exports, and attachments each year. Most people receive no formal instruction in how to organize or maintain those materials. The result is predictable: cluttered download folders, inconsistent names, duplicate files, weak backup habits, and difficulty retrieving important records when needed.

The Acrilus Digital Organization Guide provides a structured approach to organizing personal and small-team digital information. It is designed to support predictability, long-term maintainability, and low-friction retrieval at scale.

## Objectives

The framework aims to support:

- predictable placement of digital information
- rapid retrieval of important records
- reduced cognitive load in day-to-day filing decisions
- scalable organization from hundreds to tens of thousands of files
- better backup and resilience practices
- cleaner long-term archives

## Core Principles

### Predictability
A user should be able to infer where an item belongs and where it will later be found.

### Consistency
Naming conventions, folder logic, and lifecycle rules should be applied consistently rather than reinvented case by case.

### Scalability
The structure should continue to function as file counts grow and categories deepen.

### Low Cognitive Load
Good systems reduce the number of micro-decisions required during capture and filing.

### Lifecycle Awareness
Files change in relevance over time. A filing system should reflect active use, future reference, and archival retention.

## Recommended model

Acrilus uses:

- category-first organization
- deterministic file naming
- lifecycle subfolders where useful
- metadata and tagging where helpful
- backup and governance practices as part of the system, not afterthoughts

## Intended audience

This guide is suitable for:

- individuals managing complex personal records
- professionals with large digital archives
- small teams that need lightweight governance
- people migrating from ad hoc storage into a structured system
