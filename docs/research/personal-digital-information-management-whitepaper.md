# Personal Digital Information Management
## A Practical Framework for Organizing Digital Information Ecosystems

**Author:** Acrilus Project

## Abstract

The volume of digital information produced by individuals has increased dramatically over the past two decades, yet most people receive little or no formal training in how to manage digital information effectively. This paper proposes a practical framework for personal digital information management based on information architecture, deterministic naming conventions, lifecycle management, metadata, backup discipline, and security-aware design.

## Core argument

Personal digital systems increasingly contain financial, medical, legal, educational, creative, and operational records. Without structure, retrieval becomes unreliable and long-term maintenance degrades. A hybrid approach that combines hierarchy, deterministic naming, metadata, and resilience practices provides a more durable model.

## Framework elements

- category-first information architecture
- deterministic file naming
- lifecycle-based file management
- metadata and tagging
- backup and resilience
- privacy and identity segmentation

## Conclusion

Personal digital information should be treated as a designed system rather than an improvised collection of apps and folders. The Acrilus model is intended as a practical framework that can later inform dedicated software tools.
